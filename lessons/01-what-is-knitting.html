What knitting actually is

Ten minutes, no yarn, no needles. This is the lesson that makes every later instruction make sense — because once you can see what a stitch is, phrases like “knit through the back loop” stop being magic words.

10 minutes Nothing to buy yet Read it once, then skim it again after lesson 4

One loop pulled through another loop

That is the entire craft. You have a row of loops sitting on a stick. You pull a new loop of yarn through each old loop, and the new loops end up on a second stick. When you reach the end you swap the sticks around and do it again.

Everything else — cables, lace, colourwork, the shaping in a dog sweater — is variations on which loop you pull through, in which direction, and in what order.

The one sentence to remember Knitted fabric is not woven and it is not glued. It is a single continuous strand of yarn, held together entirely by the fact that each loop is trapped by the loop above it. Cut the strand anywhere and the whole thing can come undone — which is both the danger and the superpower.

Why “through the back loop” means something

The head of a stitch straddles the needle: one leg in front, one behind. Push your needle in on the front side and the stitch stays open and flat. Push it in on the back side and the stitch twists a half turn. Patterns use both — but only on purpose. If your knitting looks tight and the Vs look crossed at the base, you are twisting them by accident.

Why a dropped stitch runs

If a head slips off the needle, nothing is holding the loop below it. It pops open, which frees the loop below that, and so on down the wale — a ladder. It is not a disaster and lesson 8 shows you how to climb it back up with a crochet hook. But it is why you count.

Knit and purl are the same stitch

There are exactly two things you can do to a stitch: pull the new loop through towards you, or pull it away from you. Pull it away and you get a knit. Pull it towards you and you get a purl. Same loop, same yarn — you are just looking at the other side of it.

This is the trick behind every texture Because knit and purl are two faces of one thing, you can make patterns by choosing which face points outwards at each stitch. Alternate them by row and you get garter stitch. Alternate them by column and you get ribbing. Alternate them like a chessboard and you get seed stitch. Three completely different fabrics, two stitches, no new skills.

Why knitted fabric stretches — and why that matters for a dog

Woven cloth is two sets of straight threads crossing at right angles. Pull it and there is nowhere for the threads to go. Knitted fabric is loops, and a loop can change shape: pull sideways and the loops flatten and widen; let go and they spring back.

Relaxed — 100%

This is why a knitted sweater can go over a Basenji's head and deep chest and then sit snugly at the waist, and why a woven one would need buttons, zips or a much looser fit. It is also why gauge — how many stitches you get per 10 cm — is the single number the whole project depends on. Loops that are 10% bigger than the pattern assumed give you a sweater 10% too big. Lesson 5 deals with this properly.

The direction that does not stretch Knitting stretches a lot sideways, some vertically, and almost not at all along a cast-on or bound-off edge unless you deliberately work those edges loosely. Every "I can't get it over his head" story starts at one of those two edges.

The vocabulary you now own

WordWhat it meansWhere you will meet it
StitchOne loop.Everywhere. “Cast on 76 stitches.”
RowOne pass across flat knitting, left needle to right needle.Flat pieces — the belly panel of the sweater.
RoundOne lap of a tube. There is no turning, so you never see the back.Most of the sweater.
WaleA vertical column of stitches.Ribbing is alternating knit and purl wales.
Working yarnThe strand still attached to the ball.“Bring the working yarn to the front.”
Right side / RSThe face that will be on the outside of the finished thing.“With RS facing, pick up 40 sts.”
Wrong side / WSThe other one. Nothing is wrong with it.“End after a WS row.”
Live stitchA stitch still on a needle, able to be worked or to unravel.“Place 14 sts on hold.”
Gauge / tensionStitches and rows per 10 cm.The number that decides whether it fits.
