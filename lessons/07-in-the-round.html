Lesson 07 · The core skills

Knitting in the round

A dog sweater is a tube. You could knit it flat and sew up the side — or you could knit the tube directly, never purl a single stitch, and never sew anything. This lesson is why the project is far easier than it looks.

30 minutes Essential for the project Also: the one unfixable mistake

What changes

When you knit flat, you reach the end of the row, turn the work over, and come back the other way — so you alternate between looking at the right side and the wrong side. That is why stockinette needs a purl row: you are making knit stitches from the back.

When you knit in the round, you never turn. You spiral upwards, always facing the outside of the tube. So:

  • Every round is a knit round, and the result is stockinette. No purling at all.
  • There are no rows, only rounds, and no wrong-side rows to keep track of.
  • There is no seam to sew, and nothing to rub against a dog's ribs.
  • Charts and patterns are read from right to left every round, never reversed.

The cost is that a round has no natural start or end, so you have to mark one.

The circular needle

The cable length rule The circular needle must be shorter than the circumference of the tube you are knitting — otherwise the stitches cannot reach around and you will be fighting the needle all evening. For a sweater around 54 cm in circumference, a 40 cm circular is right. If your only needle is longer, use magic loop instead (below).

What to do when the tube is too small for the needle

Magic loop

Use a long cable (80–100 cm) and pull a loop of it out between the stitches, splitting them into two halves. Knit one half, rearrange, knit the other. One needle handles every size from a sock to a jumper. Slightly fiddly, endlessly useful.

Double-pointed needles

Stitches divided over three or four needles with a fifth doing the work. The traditional answer, still the best for the little leg cuffs at the end of this project. Looks alarming, is not.

Two circulars

Half the stitches on each of two circular needles, each half worked with its own tips. Fewer ladders than DPNs, no loop-pulling. Requires owning two needles the same size.

Joining in the round — the one mistake you cannot undo

Everything else in knitting can be fixed. This cannot. If the cast-on edge is twisted anywhere around the circle when you join, you have made a Möbius strip: the tube will have a permanent half-turn in it, and there is no way to fix it except to rip out everything and cast on again.

Two habits that make it safe First: lay the needle flat on a table before joining and run your finger along the whole cast-on edge, checking the bumpy edge faces the same way the whole way round. Second: work the first round flat — do not join — then join at the start of round two, when there is a row of knitting to show you clearly which way is up. The tiny gap this leaves is closed with the cast-on tail when you weave in your ends.

Markers and the beginning of the round

A stitch marker is a small ring that sits on the needle between two stitches. It is not attached to the knitting; when you reach it you simply slip it from the left needle to the right and carry on. Patterns abbreviate this as sm (slip marker) and pm (place marker).

You will use three in this project:

BOR (use a different colour)Start of the roundOne lap is complete. Everything in the pattern is counted from here.
ADog's right sideWhere to increase and decrease.
BDog's left sideSame, mirrored.
If a marker falls off Do not panic and do not guess. Look at the fabric: the shaping lines you have already worked are visible as diagonals running up the sides. Follow one up to the needle and put the marker back there. The beginning of the round can be found the same way, from the cast-on tail hanging at the collar.

Two things that look like mistakes but are not

The jog

Because a round is a spiral rather than a closed ring, the start of each round sits one row higher than the end. In plain stockinette this is invisible. It becomes obvious only with stripes, where the colour change makes a small step. This project is a single colour, so it does not arise — but now you know why striped hats have that little jog.

Ladders

A column of loose stitches where you moved from one needle to the next, on DPNs or magic loop. Fix: knit the first two stitches after each changeover a little more firmly, and shift where the changeover falls every few rounds so no single column takes all the strain.

Working flat inside a round

The leg openings in the sweater need you to stop going round and work back and forth for a while, on part of the stitches only. This sounds exotic; it is just flat knitting on a subset of the tube.

While you are working flat you must purl the wrong-side rows again, exactly as in lesson 4. It is the only purling in the whole garment, it lasts about twenty rows, and then you are back to the round.

Practice piece: a cowl for yourself

Cast on 100 stitches on a 40 cm circular, join carefully in the round, work 2×2 rib for 4 cm, stockinette until it is 20 cm tall, 4 cm of rib, bind off loosely. It is the sweater in miniature, minus the shaping, and it takes an evening. If you can do this, the project holds no surprises.
