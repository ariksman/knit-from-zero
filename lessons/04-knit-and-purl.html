Lesson 04 · The core skills

Knit, purl & bind off

This is the whole craft. Two stitches and one way of stopping. Every pattern ever written, including the sweater at the end of this course, is made of nothing else.

40 minutes Yarn + both needles The lesson to repeat until it feels boring

How to hold everything

There is no correct way, only two common ones. Both produce identical fabric. Try both for five minutes and keep whichever annoys you less.

English (“throwing”)

The working yarn is held in your right hand. To wrap, you let go slightly and throw the yarn around the needle. Slower per stitch, but the movement is obvious and easy to learn — most people start here.

Continental (“picking”)

The working yarn is held in your left hand, tensioned over the index finger. The right needle scoops the yarn rather than being wrapped. Faster once learned, and much easier if you already crochet.

Tension: the thing nobody explains The yarn must run through your fingers with a little resistance — enough that it does not flop, not so much that it drags. Most people wrap it once around the little finger and over the index finger. Your first hundred stitches will be too tight because you are gripping. This is normal and it fixes itself.

The knit stitch

Say the four steps out loud as you do them. It sounds silly and it works: in, wrap, through, off.

Knit every row and you get garter stitch Knit every stitch of every row on a flat piece and the fabric comes out with horizontal ridges on both sides. That is garter stitch — the easiest fabric there is, completely flat, and reversible. Two knitted rows make one visible ridge.

The purl stitch

A purl is a knit stitch worked backwards. Everything is mirrored: the yarn starts in front instead of behind, the needle goes in from the right instead of the left, and you push away instead of pulling through.

The commonest purl mistake Forgetting to bring the yarn to the front between the needles before you purl, and to the back before you knit. If you carry it over the top of the needle instead, you create an extra loop — an accidental yarn over — and your stitch count grows by one, with a small hole to prove it. If your count keeps creeping up, this is why.

Knit one row, purl the next: stockinette

Alternate the two and you get the classic smooth fabric that most garments are made of. In this course's sweater the body is stockinette — but because it is knitted in the round as a tube, you never actually purl it. Every round is a knit round, and the smooth side is always facing you. That is one of the great pleasures of circular knitting.

Binding off

Also called casting off. It closes the live stitches so the fabric cannot unravel, and it is the last thing you do to any piece.

Loosely. Really, genuinely loosely. A bound-off edge has almost no stretch of its own. On a dog sweater the hem edge has to pass over the ribcage and the collar edge over the head. Bind off with a needle two sizes larger than you knitted with, or consciously make each stitch a bit bigger. If in doubt, undo it and redo it looser — it takes five minutes and it is the difference between a garment and a decoration.

Things that will go wrong today

What you seeWhat happenedWhat to do
Stitch count grows every rowThe yarn is travelling over the needle instead of between the needles, creating accidental yarn overs — usually at the very start of a row.Check the yarn position before the first stitch of every row. Behind for knit, in front for purl.
Stitch count shrinksTwo stitches worked together by accident, or a stitch slid off the end.Count after every row for the first few rows. Lesson 8 has the rescue.
Fabric is so tight the needle will not go inGripping. Everyone does this.Consciously loosen the wrap. Do not try to solve it with bigger needles yet — it will settle on its own within an hour of knitting.
Stitches look crossed at the baseYou are entering from the wrong side and twisting them.The right needle goes into the front leg of the loop, left to right, unless told otherwise.
The yarn splits into separate pliesSharp needle tip, or spearing rather than sliding.Aim the tip along the leg of the stitch rather than at it. Blunter needles help.
One edge has big sloppy loopsThe first stitch of each row is stretched.Give the yarn a small extra tug on the second stitch of each row, not the first.

Practice piece: a coaster

Before the sweater, make something small and finished. It takes half an hour and it teaches you more than another hour of reading.

  1. Cast on 24 stitches.

    Long tail, on your 4 mm needle.

  2. Knit every row until it is square.

    Roughly 24 ridges — that is 48 rows. Measure with the tape rather than counting if you prefer.

  3. Bind off loosely and weave in the ends.

    Lesson 9 covers weaving properly, but for a coaster: thread the tail on a tapestry needle and sew it back and forth through the edge stitches for 5 cm.

Keep it. In a month you will look at it and be delighted by how bad it is.
