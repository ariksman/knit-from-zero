Lesson 06 · The core skills

Increases & decreases

A rectangle is easy. A dog is not a rectangle. Shaping is how you turn a tube into something with a neck, a ribcage and a waist — by adding and removing stitches in the right places, in a way that looks deliberate.

30 minutes Four techniques, used everywhere This is where knitting gets interesting

The idea

The width of your fabric is the number of stitches on the needle. To make the fabric wider you need more stitches; to make it narrower, fewer. An increase turns one stitch into two. A decrease turns two stitches into one.

What separates a good garment from a homemade-looking one is not whether you shape but which increase and decrease you choose, because each one leans in a direction. Used in mirrored pairs, they draw clean diagonal lines that look like they were designed. Used randomly, they look like mistakes.

Increases

kfb — knit front and back

Knit into the front of a stitch as usual but do not slide it off; then knit into the back of the same stitch and slide both new loops off. One stitch became two.

Pros: almost impossible to get wrong, no fiddling with the running bar. Cons: it leaves a small visible bump — fine inside garter or seed stitch, obvious in smooth stockinette. Perfectly acceptable if M1 is defeating you.

yo — yarn over

Simply bring the yarn over the needle before the next stitch. On the following row that loop is worked as a stitch. It creates a deliberate hole.

Use it for: lace, and for buttonholes. Do not use it for: shaping a sweater — the holes are draughty and they catch on claws. If holes are appearing in your knitting where you did not want them, you are making accidental yarn overs.

Decreases

The rule of thumb for pairing Think of the shaping line as a seam you are drawing. Both decreases should lean towards that line so the fabric folds neatly around it. In practice: ssk on the right-hand side of the line, k2tog on the left. For increases it is the mirror: M1R before the marker, M1L after it. Get this backwards and the shaping still works — it just looks slightly wrong and you will not be able to say why.

How they are used in the sweater

Both shaping sections of the Basenji sweater use the same structure: two markers sitting at the dog's sides, with a paired increase or decrease worked either side of each marker every few rounds.

Chest — increasing

[K to 1 st before marker, M1R, k1, sm, k1, M1L] twice, k to end

Four stitches added per round, two at each side. Worked every other round so the widening is gradual — increase too fast and you get a visible flare; too slowly and the sweater strangles the shoulders.

Waist — decreasing

[K to 3 sts before marker, k2tog, k1, sm, k1, ssk] twice, k to end

Four stitches removed per round, worked every fourth round — slower than the increases, because the tuck-up runs over a longer stretch of the body than the ribcage flare does.

Why there is a plain stitch either side of the marker Notice the k1 before and after each sm (slip marker). That single plain stitch on each side of the shaping keeps the increases and decreases from sitting right on top of each other, which would make a puckered, twisted line. It also gives you a visual channel to count along, so you can check your work at a glance.

The same instructions as a chart

Patterns often draw shaping instead of writing it. Each square is a stitch, read from the bottom up, and right-side rows are read from right to left — the direction you actually knit. Lesson 10 goes into this properly; here is a preview showing a decrease line.

Practise before you commit

Cast on 20 stitches and knit a small “bowtie”. It takes twenty minutes and it will show you exactly what your shaping looks like in your yarn.

  1. Knit 6 rows of stockinette.

    Purl the wrong-side rows.

  2. Decrease row: k4, ssk, k8, k2tog, k4.

    18 stitches. Purl back. Repeat this pair of rows three times, adjusting the middle count. Down to 14 stitches.

  3. Then increase back out.

    Increase row: k4, M1L, knit to last 4, M1R, k4. Purl back. Repeat until you are at 20 again.

  4. Bind off and look at it in good light.

    You should see four clean diagonal lines. If any of them look like a scar rather than a line, you have a lean the wrong way round — check which decrease is on which side.
