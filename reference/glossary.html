Abbreviations

ShortLongWhat it means
altalternateEvery other row or round.
begbeginningUsually “beg of round”.
BObind off / cast offClose the live stitches so the fabric cannot unravel.
BORbeginning of roundThe marked point where each round starts.
COcast onCreate the first row of stitches.
contcontinueCarry on doing what you were doing.
decdecreaseMake the row narrower by one or more stitches.
DPNdouble-pointed needleShort needle with a point at each end, used in sets for small tubes.
follfollowing“every foll 4th row” — every fourth row after this one.
incincreaseMake the row wider by one or more stitches.
kknitThe basic stitch: pull the new loop away from you.
k2togknit two togetherRight-leaning decrease. Knit two stitches as if they were one.
kfbknit front and backIncrease. Knit into the front then the back of the same stitch.
ktblknit through back loopEnter the stitch from the back — deliberately twists it.
M1Lmake one leftLeft-leaning increase from the running bar, lifted front to back, knitted through the back.
M1Rmake one rightRight-leaning increase from the running bar, lifted back to front, knitted through the front.
ppurlThe other basic stitch: pull the new loop towards you.
p2togpurl two togetherDecrease worked on the purl side.
pattpattern“Work in patt” — carry on with the established stitch pattern.
pmplace markerPut a stitch marker on the needle at this point.
pssopass slipped stitch overLift a slipped stitch over the one just worked and off the needle.
remremainingHow many stitches are left.
reprepeatDo it again, as many times as stated.
rndroundOne full lap of a tube.
RSright sideThe face that will show on the finished item.
slslipMove a stitch from one needle to the other without working it.
smslip markerMove the marker across as you pass it. Never knit a marker.
sskslip, slip, knitLeft-leaning decrease. Slip two knitwise, then knit them together from that position.
st / stsstitch / stitchesThe unit of everything.
St ststockinette / stocking stitchKnit on the right side, purl on the wrong side. Smooth face, curling edges.
tblthrough back loopWork into the back leg of the stitch instead of the front.
togtogetherAs in k2tog.
WSwrong sideThe face that will be inside.
wyib / wyifwith yarn in back / frontWhere the working yarn must sit while you slip a stitch.
yoyarn overWrap the yarn over the needle to make a new stitch and a deliberate hole.

Terms

TermMeaning
Ball bandThe paper wrapper on a ball of yarn. Carries the fibre content, weight, length, suggested gauge and needle, colour, dye lot and washing symbols.
BlockingWashing a finished piece and drying it in the shape you want. Evens out the stitches and sets the measurements.
CourseA horizontal line of stitches — a row.
Dye lotThe batch a ball was dyed in. Balls from different lots can differ visibly. Buy one lot.
EaseThe difference between the body and the garment. Positive ease is roomier, negative ease is tighter than the body and relies on stretch.
FroggingRipping work out. “Rip it, rip it.”
Garter stitchKnit every row. Ridged, flat, reversible, does not curl.
Gauge / tensionStitches and rows per 10 cm. The number every other number in a pattern depends on.
JogThe small step where a round ends one row lower than it began. Only visible in stripes.
LadderA column of loose horizontal strands, either from a dropped stitch or from loose joins between needles in the round.
LifelineA contrasting thread run through a whole row of live stitches so you can safely rip back to it.
Live stitchA stitch still on a needle or a holder — able to be worked, and able to unravel.
Magic loopKnitting a small tube on one long circular needle by pulling a loop of cable out between the stitches.
Mattress stitchAn invisible seam worked from the right side between two vertical edges.
Negative easeA garment measurement smaller than the body it goes on, relying on the fabric's stretch. Used for the collar of the sweater.
Running barThe short horizontal strand between two neighbouring stitches. M1 increases are made from it.
Seed / moss stitchKnit and purl alternating in both directions. Thick, flat, and slow.
SelvedgeThe edge stitch of a flat piece, often worked differently to make seaming or picking up easier.
SuperwashWool treated so its scales cannot interlock, which stops it felting and makes it machine washable. Essential for a dog garment.
SwatchA test square knitted, washed and measured to establish gauge before starting.
TinkUnknitting one stitch at a time. “Knit” backwards.
Tuck-upThe upward curve of a dog's belly behind the ribcage. Pronounced in Basenjis, and the reason this pattern has waist shaping.
WaleA vertical column of stitches.
Working yarnThe strand still attached to the ball, as opposed to the tail.
WPIWraps per inch. A way to identify the weight of an unlabelled yarn by wrapping it round a ruler.
YokeThe section of a top-down garment between the neck and the point where the body becomes a straight tube. Here, the chest increases.
