Troubleshooting index

Organised by what you are actually looking at, not by what it is called. Search for the symptom.

While you are knitting

What you seeWhyWhat to do
Stitch count creeps up every rowAccidental yarn overs — the yarn is going over the needle rather than between the needles when you swap between knit and purl, usually at the start of a row.Check the yarn position before the first stitch of every row: behind for knit, in front for purl. Lesson 4
Stitch count dropsTwo stitches worked together by accident, or one slid off the needle.Look down the fabric for a ladder. If there is none, restore the count with an M1 on the next round. Lesson 8
A hole where you did not want oneAn accidental yarn over, or an M1 worked without twisting the new stitch closed.M1 must be knitted through the back loop (M1L) or the front (M1R). Tink back and redo. Lesson 6
Fabric so tight the needle barely goes inGripping. Everyone does this for the first hour.Loosen the wrap consciously. Do not switch to bigger needles yet — it settles by itself. Lesson 4
Some stitches look crossed at the baseTwisted stitches — you are entering from the wrong side of the loop.The needle goes into the front leg, left to right, unless the pattern says tbl. Lesson 8
The yarn keeps splitting into pliesSharp needle tips, or spearing the stitch rather than sliding into it.Aim along the leg of the stitch, not at it. Blunter tips help.
A whole column of loose strands up the fabricA dropped stitch that has run.Secure the loose loop, then hook each rung back up with a crochet hook. Lesson 8
A vertical line of loose stitches in a tubeLadders where you change needles in the round.Work the first two stitches after each changeover more firmly, and move where the changeover falls. Lesson 7
One edge of a flat piece has big sloppy loopsThe first stitch of each row gets stretched.Tug the yarn on the second stitch of the row, not the first.
The k2/p2 columns have drifted out of lineStitch count changed, or yarn carried over the needle between knits and purls.Count. 2×2 rib needs a multiple of 4. Lesson 5
A stitch marker has fallen offIt happens.Follow the shaping line already worked up the fabric to the needle and put the marker back there. Lesson 7
The tube has a permanent half-twist in itThe cast-on edge was twisted when you joined in the round.There is no fix. Rip out and cast on again — and this time lay it flat and check the whole circle first. Lesson 7

When the fabric looks wrong

What you seeWhyWhat to do
Stockinette curls into a tube at the edgesNormal and unavoidable — knit and purl stitches are different sizes.Nothing while knitting. Blocking flattens it, and every edge in the pattern already has ribbing or garter on it. Lesson 5
Ribbing looks stretched out and does not spring backWorked on needles that were too big, or the yarn has no elasticity (cotton, alpaca).Rib on needles one size smaller than the body, and use wool for anything that must grip.
A visible horizontal line where you joined a new ballDifferent dye lot.Nothing now. Next time buy all the yarn at once from one lot. Lesson 2
The shaping lines look like scars rather than linesThe decreases are leaning away from the shaping line instead of towards it.ssk on the right of the line, k2tog on the left. Lesson 6
A band of looser stitches across several rowsTension changed — a different evening, a different mood, or a different chair.Blocking hides most of it. Consistency comes with time.
The stitches look uneven and bumpy overallOrdinary beginner tension.Block it. The improvement is genuinely startling. Lesson 9

When the finished sweater does not fit

What you seeWhyWhat to do
It will not go over the headThe cast-on edge is too tight — almost never the ribbing itself.Undo the cast-on edge and redo it over two needles held together. Lesson 3
It will not go over the ribcageThe bind-off at the hem is too tight.Undo the bind-off and redo it loosely, or with a stretchy bind-off. Lesson 4
It is about 10% too big or too small everywhereYour gauge was not the gauge you entered. This is almost always the answer.Re-swatch, wash the swatch, and regenerate the pattern. Lesson 5
It slides backwards and bunches behind the shouldersCollar too loose, or too much ease at the chest.Cast on fewer collar stitches; reduce chest ease to 0. Pattern
It rotates around the dog on a walkToo much chest ease.Reduce ease to 0 and re-knit, or add a grippy strip inside the belly panel. Knit-along
It gapes at the leg openingsOpenings too deep.Add the ribbed leg cuffs — they usually solve it entirely. If not, reduce the opening rows by 4 next time.
The dog walks stiffly in itOpenings too shallow, or too close together.Widen the openings and check the belly bridge width. Knit-along
It rides up over the hipsToo long — the hind legs are pushing it forward.Reduce the back length input by 3–4 cm.
The belly gets wet or soiledMale dog, full-length belly panel.Turn on the belly-notch option. Variations
It grew a size in the washSuperwash merino relaxes when washed. Your swatch was measured unwashed.Always wash the swatch. To recover: it will shrink back slightly if dried flat and patted to size. Lesson 9
It felted into a solid boardUntreated wool, warm water, agitation.Not reversible. Use superwash for anything that will be machine washed. Lesson 2
The three questions that solve most problems 1 · Have you counted? Most mysterious problems are a stitch count that drifted twenty rows ago.
2 · Did you wash the swatch? Most fit problems are an unwashed swatch.
3 · Can you see it from a metre away? If not, it is not a problem.
