Measure your dog

Five numbers. Ten minutes. A soft tape measure and a handful of treats. Get these right and the sweater fits; guess them and no amount of good knitting will save it.

10 minutes Soft tape measure Measure a standing dog, not a lying one

How to take each one

  1. A · Neck circumference

    Around the base of the neck, where a collar naturally sits — not up under the jaw. Snug enough that the tape does not fall, loose enough to slide two fingers underneath.

    Why it matters: this sets the collar. Too tight and the sweater will not go over the head; too loose and it slides back and bunches at the shoulders. The pattern applies about 8% negative ease here on purpose, because ribbing stretches.

  2. B · Chest girth — the important one

    All the way around the body at the deepest point, immediately behind the front legs. Keep the tape perpendicular to the spine and let it sit flat against the coat without compressing it.

    Why it matters: this is the widest part of the garment and the number the whole pattern is built from. Measure it three times. Basenjis have a deep, narrow, keel-shaped chest, so this number is usually larger than people expect from how slim the dog looks.

  3. C · Back length

    Along the spine, from the base of the neck (the bump between the shoulder blades, where the collar sat for measurement A) to the base of the tail. The dog must be standing square, head up.

    Why it matters: this sets the length. The pattern makes the sweater about 93% of this, so that it covers the body without interfering with the tail — which on a Basenji curls tightly over the back and should not be sat on by knitwear.

  4. D · Waist girth optional

    Around the body at the narrowest point, in front of the back legs. If you skip this, the pattern estimates it as 84% of the chest, which is right for most Basenjis.

  5. E · Front leg optional

    Around the top of the front leg, right up in the armpit. Used only as a sanity check on the leg openings — the pattern sizes them from the chest measurement.

Three ways people get this wrong Measuring a sitting or lying dog. The chest changes shape completely. Stand them up.
Pulling the tape tight. Snug, not compressed. You are measuring the dog, not the skeleton.
Measuring over a harness or thick collar. Take everything off first.

Reference numbers for a Basenji

The breed standard puts an adult Basenji at 40–43 cm at the shoulder and 9.5–11 kg, with a deep chest, a short level back and a pronounced tuck-up. Here is the range you should expect — if your measurements fall well outside it, measure again before you cast on.

MeasurementFemale, ~9.5 kgTypical, ~10 kgMale, ~11 kg
A Neck33–35 cm36 cm37–39 cm
B Chest girth49–52 cm54 cm56–60 cm
C Back length38–41 cm42 cm43–46 cm
D Waist41–44 cm45 cm47–50 cm
E Front leg13–15 cm15 cm15–17 cm
Why a Basenji needs a shaped sweater and a Labrador does not The ratio of chest to waist on a Basenji is roughly 54:45 — a 9 cm drop over about 15 cm of body. That is sighthound-adjacent proportions, and it is why a straight tube slides forward and bunches at the shoulders on this breed. The waist decreases in this pattern exist entirely because of that drop. A barrel-chested breed can get away with a rectangle; this one cannot.

A note on ease

Ease is the difference between the dog and the garment. This pattern uses:

PlaceEaseWhy
Collar−8%Ribbing must grip. It stretches over the head and springs back onto the neck.
Chest+1 cmJust enough room to breathe and move the shoulders. Any more and the sweater rotates around the dog.
Waist0Follows the body exactly, so it does not swing.
Length−7%Stops short of the tail so nothing is sat on or soiled.

If your dog is between sizes, round the chest up and the length down. A slightly roomy sweater is comfortable; a slightly long one gets in the way.
