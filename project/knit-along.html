The knit-along

The same pattern, walked through slowly, in the order you will actually meet it. Each stage says what you are doing, what it should look like when it is right, what usually goes wrong, and what to check before you carry on.

Evening zero

Before the sweater: the swatch

This is not optional and it is not part of the sweater. Cast on about 34 stitches, work a 15 cm square with garter borders, bind off, wash it, dry it flat, measure it. Then type the two numbers into the generator.

Time: forty minutes plus overnight drying. Why: it is the only thing standing between you and a sweater that fits a different dog. See lesson 5.

Evening one · about 2 hours

Stage 1 — the collar

Cast on 72 stitches with the smaller needles, using the long-tail method and a tail of about two metres. Drop a marker on the needle every 20 stitches as you go so you can count them without losing your place.

Spread them out, lay the needle on a table, and check the whole circle for a twist. Then place the beginning-of-round marker and join.

Work *k2, p2 around, for 24 rounds — about 8 cm. It will feel slow. Ribbing always does.

Looks right when You can see clean vertical columns: two knit stitches standing forward, two purl stitches sunk behind. The tube pulls in on itself and springs back when stretched.
Usually goes wrong because The yarn is being carried over the needle instead of between the needles when swapping between knit and purl. This makes an accidental yarn over and the rib drifts out of alignment. If your k2/p2 columns have gone crooked, that is why.

Stage 2 — widening over the chest

Change to the larger needles. On the next round, place marker A after 18 stitches and marker B after another 36. These sit at the dog's two sides.

Then alternate: an increase round, a plain round, an increase round, a plain round — 12 increase rounds in all, 23 rounds total, ending on an increase round. Every increase round adds 4 stitches, so you go 72 → 76 → 80 … → 120.

Work 5 more plain rounds. The piece should now measure about 9 cm below the collar and 55 cm around.

Looks right when Four faint diagonal lines run up from the collar towards the two markers — two at each side. Held up, the piece looks like a lampshade, not a cylinder.
Usually goes wrong because M1L and M1R get swapped, or the increases land right against the marker instead of with a plain stitch between. Both still work; both look slightly untidy. If you notice at round 30, carry on — consistency matters more than which lean you picked.
Stop and count now You should have exactly 120 stitches. Count them in tens. If you are one or two out, add or remove them on the next plain round, spaced apart — nobody will ever find them. If you are five or more out, something structural has gone wrong; go back and look at the shaping lines.

Evening two · about 2 hours

Stage 3 — the leg openings

This is the fiddly part, and it is one round plus about eighteen rows of flat knitting. Thread a lifeline first.

  1. Divide.

    Slip 15 stitches onto scrap yarn. Knit 14 (the belly bridge). Slip 15 more onto scrap yarn. Knit 76 (the back panel). Turn.

    You now have three separate things: two little bundles of held stitches, and the needle holding the bridge and the back panel.

  2. Work the back panel flat.

    Purl back across the 76 stitches, then knit, then purl, alternating, until 18 rows have been worked counting the divide round as row 1. End after a purl row. Break the yarn.

    This is the only stockinette purling in the whole garment. It will feel odd after all those knit rounds. It lasts about twenty minutes.

  3. Work the belly bridge flat.

    Rejoin the yarn at the bridge edge nearest the beginning-of-round marker and knit every row — garter stitch — until the bridge is as tall as the back panel, about 6 cm. Stop with the yarn back at that same edge, ready for a right-side row.

    Garter is used here on purpose: 14 stitches of flat stockinette would curl into a tube and sit uncomfortably in the dog's armpit.

  4. Rejoin the round.

    Knit the 14 bridge stitches, cast on 15 stitches over the first gap with the backward-loop method, knit the 76 back stitches, cast on 15 over the second gap. Replace the beginning-of-round marker. Back to 120 stitches, and back to knitting in the round.

Looks right when Two neat rectangular holes, roughly 7 cm wide and 6 cm deep, one at each side, with the narrow garter strip running between them along what will be the dog's chest.
Usually goes wrong because The two panels end up different heights, so the rejoining round pulls on one side. Measure both, do not trust the row count. If they are more than one row apart, add a row to the shorter one before rejoining.

Stage 4 — the waist

Place markers at the middle of each cast-on gap. Then work a decrease round followed by three plain rounds, five times over — 20 rounds, 20 stitches gone, down to 100.

This is where the sweater stops being a tube and starts being a Basenji. Hold it up after the third decrease round and you should be able to see the shape appear.

Evening three · about 2 hours

Stage 5 — straight, then the hem

Thirty-six plain rounds. This is the easy, pleasant, television part of the project. Measure rather than count: knit until the piece is about 34 cm from the bottom of the collar.

Then change to the smaller needles and work *k2, p2 for 15 rounds, about 5 cm. Bind off very loosely in pattern — knit the knits and purl the purls as you go, and use a needle two sizes larger if in any doubt.

Stage 6 — cuffs, ends, blocking

  1. Leg cuffs.

    Put the 15 held stitches of one opening back on the smaller needles. Pick up about 13 stitches down one side, 15 along the cast-on edge, and 13 up the other side — about 56 stitches. Join, work 4 rounds of k2/p2 rib, bind off loosely. Repeat on the other side.

  2. Weave in every end.

    Five centimetres forward along a column of stitches, then two centimetres back on the neighbouring column. No knots. See lesson 9.

  3. Reinforce the leg-opening corners.

    A few duplicate stitches over each of the four corners, on the wrong side, with the tail you already have there. Two minutes, and it is where the sweater would otherwise fail first.

  4. Block it.

    Cool water, wool wash, 20 minutes' soak, roll in a towel, pat out flat to the finished measurements, dry for a day. Do not stretch the collar.

Fitting it, and what to change next time

What you see on the dogWhat to change
Slides backwards, gathers behind the shouldersCollar is too loose. Next time cast on 4–8 fewer stitches, or work the collar on a needle one size smaller.
Will not go over the headThe cast-on edge, not the collar itself. Redo it over two needles held together.
Rotates around the body during a walkToo much ease at the chest. Reduce the ease setting to 0 and re-knit — or accept it and add a small strip of grippy fabric inside the belly panel.
Gapes at the leg openingsOpenings are too deep. Reduce the leg-opening rows by 4 next time.
Binds at the shoulder, dog walks stifflyOpenings are too shallow or too far apart. Widen them, and check the belly bridge is not too wide.
Rides up at the backToo long, catching on the hind legs. Shorten by working fewer plain rounds.
Boy dog, wet bellyTurn on the belly-notch option in the generator and re-work the last section.
Fit it damp The one moment you can change the size is while it is still wet from blocking. Try it on the dog for thirty seconds — briefly, and then take it off — and note where it pulls. Then pat it out on the towel in the direction it needs. Wool holds the shape you dry it in.

Finished?

Then you have gone from never having held a needle to a shaped, seamless, correctly-fitting garment, and you now know enough to knit almost anything — a hat is this project without the leg openings, a jumper is this project with sleeves.
